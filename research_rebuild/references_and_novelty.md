# References and novelty audit

The manuscript's 15 references were checked against Crossref records; raw matches and retrieval failures are preserved under `manifests/reference_*.json`, and the automatic comparison is in `tables/reference_audit.csv`. Automatic title matching alone does not establish the correct article or edition. In particular, the automated Lu match was an evaluation record, and the Kipf match was a different paper. These were rejected and resolved using primary records below.

Manual resolutions: [Girvan & Newman 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12060727/) DOI 10.1073/pnas.122653799; [Kipf & Welling, ICLR 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907); [Kolaczyk 2009](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-88146-1); [Little & Rubin, second edition 2002](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781119013563); [Lu et al. 2005](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature03702); [Marbach et al. 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nmeth.2016); [McCullagh & Nelder, second edition 1989](https://www.routledge.com/Generalized-Linear-Models-Second-Edition/McCullagh-Nelder/p/book/9780412317606); [Langfelder & Horvath 2008](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-9-559). The Barrett paper's 2012 online date and 2013 journal-volume date are not inherently conflicting. The remaining exact title/DOI matches appear in the automatic table; no fabricated citation was identified, but contextual support is more limited than the paper implies.

Lu and Volinia support the importance of miRNA expression in cancer, not a validated ceRNA mechanism or dropout model. Basso and Marbach study regulatory-network inference and benchmarking; neither, simply by being cited, establishes a zero adjusted-EBC effect or proves that a stochastic block model is an invalid conditional null for this estimand. Missing-data textbooks and Troyanskaya's imputation paper do not demonstrate that retaining processed values is unbiased or that any amount of ties necessarily invalidates Spearman correlation. The GNN and Louvain references are background; no corrected classifier or module algorithm is evaluated here. Generalized-linear-model references justify the mean model family, not one-endpoint-clustered biological inference.

The hard/soft distinction predates this work. [Zhang & Horvath 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16646834/) developed a weighted coexpression framework, and [WGCNA 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2631488/) provides weighted correlation analysis software. [Langfelder et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001057) addresses module preservation/reproducibility. Those foundations mean that reduced variability of continuous weights is not sufficient novelty by itself.

Directly relevant uncertainty/reproducibility work includes [Co-expression networks: graph properties and topological comparisons](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2804297/), which uses bootstrap microarray resampling; [Stability Indicators in Network Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1654), which applies subsampling stability to a miRNA dataset; and [BINCO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5028), which develops bootstrap-based edge selection/FDR methodology for graphical models. Their estimands differ from retained-edge dropout in a hard-thresholded marginal correlation graph.

A current directly relevant preprint, [Uncertainty-Aware Gene Rankings Reveal Key Players in Coexpression Networks](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2025.12.13.694156v1.full), explicitly studies uncertainty-aware centrality rankings and reproducibility; its preprint status must be stated. [Aggregation of recount3 RNA-seq data improves inference of consensus and tissue-specific gene coexpression networks](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280808.125) studies cross-study aggregation and network inference. These are useful comparisons for the biological application, not independent confirmation of this miRNA result. No claim of an exhaustive systematic literature review is made.

For dependence, [Cameron & Miller, Robust Inference for Dyadic Data](https://cameron.econ.ucdavis.edu/research/dyadic_cameron_miller_december2014_with_tables.pdf) addresses pairs sharing either endpoint. Even that dependence model does not by itself cover all correlations induced by shared patient samples across disjoint edges. The rebuild therefore reports complete-resample Monte Carlo covariance and withholds unsupported population inference.

Potential contribution: a traceable, empirically validated account of how threshold proximity and correlation uncertainty explain dropout, whether EBC adds reproducible predictive information, and how those choices affect named-miRNA hub reproducibility. Population EBC calibration remains a substantive missing component. No journal selection or submission strategy has been undertaken.
